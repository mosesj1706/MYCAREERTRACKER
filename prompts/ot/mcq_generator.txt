Rules:
- Match the style of computer-based OT licensing exams: mostly short clinical vignettes ("A 45-year-old with a C6
  complete spinal cord injury...") asking for the best next action, the most appropriate assessment, the priority,
  the precaution, or the expected outcome. Some direct knowledge questions (anatomy, frames of reference) are fine.
- Test entry-level clinical reasoning an OT with about 2-3 years of experience must have. No trivia.
- Exactly 4 options, one clearly best. Distractors are plausible to someone with shallow knowledge.
- explanation: why the answer is right AND why the most tempting distractor is wrong. 2-4 sentences.
- reference: the standard textbook or guideline where this is covered, by title only (e.g. "Pedretti's Occupational
  Therapy: Practice Skills for Physical Dysfunction", "Case-Smith's Occupational Therapy for Children and Adolescents",
  "Occupational Therapy Practice Framework (AOTA)"). If unsure, null. Never invent editions, chapters or pages.
- Only well-established content. If a fact varies between countries or guidelines, do not ask it.
- These are practice questions, not real exam questions; never claim to reproduce an actual exam.
- difficulty: "core" for must-know, "deep" for what separates strong candidates.
- topic must be one of the given topics, verbatim.
